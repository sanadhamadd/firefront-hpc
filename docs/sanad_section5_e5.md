## 5. Results – part 3: decomposition (E5)

Draft by Sanad: input for Alejandro, who puts §5 together. Target ≈ 0.4 page. Source: `results/tables/e5_summary.csv` (51 jobs, commit `674dab1`, `tools/plot_e5.py`); median of 3 jobs, min–max in brackets. All 51 runs gave the reference checksum.

**Strips vs blocks (E5).** We ran the MPI version with one thread per rank on the 8192² grid, splitting it either into horizontal strips (P × 1 ranks) or into blocks (`MPI_Dims_create`), with 1–32 ranks on one node and 8, 16 and 32 ranks spread evenly over two nodes.

On **one node** the MPI version scales almost ideally: from 2 to 32 ranks, efficiency stays between 0.94 and 1.01. (It can pass 1.0 because the one-rank MPI run, 78.39 s, is already 4 % faster than serial E1; speed-ups are still taken against E1.) With 32 ranks, blocks need **2.62 s** (2.58–2.85 s), a speed-up of 31.3 and efficiency 0.98, and strips 2.72 s (2.62–2.76 s), efficiency 0.94. Blocks are a little faster at every rank count, and at 16–32 ranks they spend about 11 % of the run in the halo exchange against about 14 % for strips. The reason is the amount of data: a strip sends two whole rows of 8192 cells per step, while a 32-rank block (a 4 × 8 grid of ranks, 2048 × 1024 cells each) sends four shorter edges, 6,144 cells in total, 2.7 × less. Both are small messages, so the difference stays small. This refutes our hypothesis that strips would win at this scale because the exchange is latency-bound.

On **two nodes** the picture changes. Efficiency drops to **0.47–0.60**, and the halo exchange takes **43–56 %** of the run; 32 ranks as blocks need 4.56 s (4.43–5.66 s) instead of 2.62 s on one node. The compute imbalance (slowest rank's compute time over the average) rises from 1.02–1.11 to 1.48–1.60. The second node's CPU is not the cause: 8 ranks on julia alone took 10.72 s against 10.54 s on haskell (job 33981). Most of the cost is the link between the nodes, which uses TCP because Open MPI's faster UCX layer crashed at start-up (§4). Blocks still beat strips at 16 and 32 ranks (8.87 vs 10.82 s, 4.56 vs 4.98 s), but not at 8 (17.55 vs 17.02 s).

Solid: one node; dashed: two nodes. Median of 3.

**What this means.** Within one node, the choice of decomposition hardly matters and either one scales well. Crossing the node boundary costs far more than any decomposition choice: half of the run is spent exchanging halos and waiting for neighbours. This is why the optimisation (E7, §7) targets the halo exchange, and why two-node results are only compared within one campaign (§4: the same 32-rank, two-node configuration took 3.13 s in the later E3 and E7 campaigns).
